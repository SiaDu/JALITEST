Scene:
About a year after Hamnet's death, Will has returned to the family home. He has brought gifts and has acquired a large new house for the family. He asks Agnes what she thinks of it.

Story:
Will spends much of his life working in London while Agnes and the children remain in Stratford. Their son Hamnet died while Will was away. The loss has severely strained their marriage. They are both still grieving, but they have experienced that grief largely apart from one another.

Character:
WILL carries guilt over his absence and remains obsessively preoccupied with where Hamnet has gone after death. He is also deeply attached to his life and work away from home.
AGNES remains immersed in the loss of Hamnet and resents the distance that has developed between herself and Will.

Performance Context:
The conversation begins with an apparently practical question about the new house but quickly exposes unresolved grief and marital resentment. Both characters are wounded rather than simply angry. Will attempts to express his own grief and apologize; Agnes partly recognizes their shared loss but continues to challenge the way he has withdrawn into another part of his life.
